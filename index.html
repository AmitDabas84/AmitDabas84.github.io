Why Lakehouse projects fail

I've built Lakehouse platforms for enterprise clients across travel, consulting, and logistics. Some failed. Never because of Databricks vs Fabric vs Snowflake.

The pattern: 6 months picking the platform, 3 months building medallion layers, 0 days deciding who owns the data.

The fix: Start with decisions. Assign business owners Day 1. Quality gates before Gold. Adoption alongside architecture. Lineage from the start.

Architecture without governance is just expensive storage.

Governance is Day 1, not Phase 2

At one engagement — textbook architecture, clean Delta tables, optimized Spark. Governance planned for "Phase 2."

Phase 2 never came. 200+ Gold tables, nobody knew which were trustworthy. A VP saw contradicting numbers in a board meeting. Career moment you don't forget.

Retrofitting cost 3x. Every greenfield build since starts with governance. Deliberate strategic decision.

The BI adoption problem

Most teams fix low adoption by building better dashboards. Almost never works.

At one firm, 20% to 52% across 500+ users. We embedded analytics into workflows, sat with teams for 90 days, designed around decision moments. Training on use cases, not tool features.

Adoption isn't a design problem. It's a workflow integration problem.

What gold_customers_v3_final taught me

Every engineer has seen gold_customers_v3_final. The "v3" — rebuilt twice. "Final" — promised to stop. Didn't.

6 months in, nobody remembers what tables represent. Which sources feed them? What logic is embedded?

Now I implement lineage from Day 1. The cost compounds every week. By month 6, you're doing archaeology.
